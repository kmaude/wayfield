# Wayfield Marketing Launch

Build a marketing website for "Wayfield", an AI-driven marketing platform sold to small businesses (local services, restaurants, professional services), with a friendly, non-techy, human tone. This is a lead-generation and content site only, no live account signup or authentication needed yet, the "Get started" page just has a qualifying contact form.

BRAND SYSTEM
- Name: Wayfield
- Logo mark: three forward-pointing arrow/chevron shapes layered left to right, colored indigo (#1B2A4A), blue (#2F6FED), and coral (#FF6B4A), next to the wordmark "wayfield" in lowercase.
- Colors: indigo #1B2A4A (primary, nav, headings, dark surfaces), blue #2F6FED (secondary accent), coral #FF6B4A (primary CTA buttons only, used sparingly so it stays meaningful), warm cream #F7F2EA and bone #EDE3D3 (backgrounds), white surfaces for cards, slate #6B7280 and warm brown-gray #5C5449 for body text.
- Fonts: "Sora" (600/700 weight) for headings, "Manrope" (400/500/600) for body and UI text, both loaded from Google Fonts.
- Buttons: pill-shaped (fully rounded), not sharp rectangles.
- Overall feel: warm, approachable, plain-spoken, NOT a typical cold SaaS/tech dashboard look. Avoid gradients, neon, glow, or overly "startup" visual cliches. Should feel like a trustworthy small business partner, not enterprise software.
- Never mention the underlying technology "madSense" anywhere, and avoid leading with the word "AI" in headlines; describe things by outcome instead (e.g. "handles the busywork" rather than "AI-powered automation"), except in the two FAQ/How-it-works answers noted below where acknowledging smart automated technology directly is fine.

IMPORTANT — IMAGE PLACEHOLDERS
Real photography (human, candid, warm small-business photography) will be added later. For now, every photo slot described below should be a clearly labeled placeholder: a solid warm-toned block (use color #8C7358) with a small photo icon and centered label text reading "Photo placeholder — image coming later". For full-width hero-style photo sections, add a semi-transparent navy overlay (#1B2A4A at about 55% opacity) over the placeholder block so it's obvious this is where a photo-with-text-overlay will eventually sit, and keep the heading/subhead/CTA text rendered in white on top of it exactly as it would sit on a real photo. For smaller "light" or "framed" photo slots, just use a smaller rounded placeholder block with the same icon and label, no overlay needed since there's no text on top of those.
